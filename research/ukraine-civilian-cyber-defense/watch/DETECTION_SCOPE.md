# Proactive civilian defense and coordinated disruption

## Actions within an authorized defender's scope

- Review current, corroborated threat intelligence and known attack techniques.
- Harden and observe systems the defender controls or is explicitly authorized to manage.
- Stage narrowly scoped, expiring protective DNS/firewall/EDR rules at the defender's own boundary.
- Investigate the defender's own telemetry for early compromise signals.
- Notify an affected host, registrar, service provider, CERT, or law-enforcement contact when there is evidence of malicious use of infrastructure.
- Ask for written authorization before analyzing a third party's endpoint or private logs.

## Actions not supported by this project

- Exploiting, disrupting, flooding, or degrading Russian or suspected adversary-owned systems.
- Unauthorized scans of infrastructure, credential attempts, remote malware execution, stolen-data access, or breaking into third-party accounts.
- Falsely claiming to represent the Ukrainian government or a security responder.
- Countrywide, providerwide, or ASN-wide blocks as substitutes for observed malicious indicators.

**Important:** a Russian-actor attribution does not imply a domain or server is Russian-owned. Operations can use compromised third-party services. Preserve source, date, confidence, owner/collateral-risk analysis, approval, and expiration for defensive restrictions. Incident reports should contain redacted evidence and omit unnecessary personal information.
