## Proposed one-sentence mission

Haven / PROJECT ASTRA is a personal and cooperative intelligence system through which people can deliberately extend perception, memory, understanding, imagination and action across digital and physical environments while retaining privacy, authorship and control.

Haven's conversation, an AR view, a haptic cue, an engineering workspace and a drone observation are different interfaces or capabilities of the cumulative project—not competing replacements for its mission. ## Durable principles

### A1. Extend the person; do not merely automate a screen

Judge progress by useful human abilities: remembering the relevant decision, understanding a scene, communicating intentionally, testing an idea, completing a task or obtaining a missing observation. Agent count, screenshots and model size are not the objective.

### A2. Shared reference is not shared everything

Two people may refer to the same object or place while having different private notes, histories, goals and permitted views. Joint attention can be shared without merging private memory. Each person controls enrollment, withdrawal and deliberate disclosure.

### A3. The world model is a revisable belief/evidence system

Retain sources, revisions, native locators, frames, clocks, coverage, uncertainty and contradictions. An attractive reconstruction is one view, not an all-knowing truth object. Facts can become stale or corrected. Missing evidence stays missing.

### A4. Imagination is valuable because it is distinct from observation

Generated worlds and predicted outcomes may support creativity, explanation and rehearsal. Preserve their scenario identity and assumptions. A hypothetical object or simulated event must not silently become a current observation, measured dimension or action permit.

### A5. New senses require useful mappings, not just more sensors

Connect a measured environmental quantity or deliberately selected signal to an understandable output. Prefer low-friction private cues where they help, with ordinary controls and accessible alternatives. Do not infer consent, identity, emotional truth or commands from ambiguous physiology.

### A6. Embodiment is plural

The human, phone, stationary sensor, handheld accessory, robot and aircraft are complementary ways to obtain information or perform an authorized task. Use the simplest useful embodiment first without deleting the harder paths.

### A7. Learning means evidence changes the system

Preserve failed designs, corrections, negative experiments and domain limits. Feed measured outcomes back into engineering and task planning. Do not equate self-generated evaluations or reviewer agreement with physical evidence.

### A8. Continuity must be explicit and portable

The project should not depend on a particular chat remembering everything or on one model/provider. Durable decisions, versioned source references, capability records and small task-specific context packets carry continuity.

### A9. Human attention and independence are design resources

A system that interrupts constantly, hides uncertainty, makes exit difficult or replaces the person's choices with opaque decisions has missed the goal. Measure distraction, false cues, correction effort and retained manual capability along with speed.

### A10. Build ambitious capability in useful increments

A local notes/image prototype is a foundation, not the final vision. A speculative frontier is retained with a re-entry condition, not claimed implemented. Avoid both endless architecture and broad unvalidated deployment.

## Questions every future handoff must answer

Which human ability does this advance? Which retained anchors does it preserve? What observation supports it? What is inferred or simulated? Who may access or act on it? What changed since the previous version? What is the smallest useful next test? Which appealing claim is still unsupported?

Do not turn this checklist into a new mandatory research phase before ordinary authorized coding. Its purpose is to prevent drift with a small amount of durable context.
