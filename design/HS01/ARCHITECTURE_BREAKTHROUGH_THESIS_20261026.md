# Architecture breakthrough thesis for Haven — 2026-10-26

**Classification:** PUBLIC_RESEARCH + reading of Haven's saved receipts. No model was run, no GPU used, nothing downloaded or changed.
**Convention:** **[S]** = source claim (link given; "2ndary" = I only saw a blog/aggregator, not the primary). **[I]** = my inference. Numbers marked *predicted* are guesses with stated reasoning, not measurements.

## 1. Thesis

1. **No surveyed architecture, by itself, gives frontier capability on 8 GB VRAM + 64 GB RAM.** The one design family that decouples *knowledge capacity* (cheap, RAM/SSD-resident) from *per-token compute* (small, GPU-resident) is **hybrid linear-attention + fine-grained MoE + Engram-style lookup memory**. Haven already runs a member of it (Strata's Qwen3.8-Flash-Next). [I]
2. **On Haven today the binding constraint is the I/O path, not the architecture.** 33.02 GiB in 836 s is 40.4 MiB/s, which is what a USB 2.0 link delivers [S: Haven receipt, [linux-strata-result-20261026.json](../../ops/haven-core/linux-strata-result-20261026.json); [EXP-01](../../review/HS01/EXPERIMENT_01_SLINK_COLD_START_DIAGNOSIS.md) correctly warns this is *effective load rate*, not proven raw drive speed]. Every model Haven loads pays this tax (a 21 GB Q4 35B-A3B would take ~8 min at 40 MiB/s). [I]
3. **Tool reliability is mostly post-training, not architecture.** The 4B (76/110) beat the 125B-class teacher (55/110); both substitute a proposal for an execution request (6 cases for the 4B, 0/40 execution-refusal for Strata); oracle-SFT matched teacher-SFT at 38/110 ([RESULTS.md](../../labs/intelligence-bakeoff/distillation/tool_sft/RESULTS.md)). A bigger or exotic backbone will not fix a contract-following habit. [I]

**Correction to the brief:** "80B" for Strata's model is not supported. NVIDIA's NeMo docs list Qwen3.8-Flash-Next as ~125B backbone / 6B active / 512 experts top-10 / 48 layers (36 Gated DeltaNet + 12 sparse-attention) plus a 51.2B Engram n-gram table ([NVIDIA docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/automodel/model-coverage/large-language-models/qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next.md), 2ndary; French press gives release 2026-08-26, license `qwen-community-1.0`, 2ndary). Sanity check [I]: 125B × ~2.3 bpw ≈ 36 GB, matching Strata's "35.5 GB experts in RAM" for IQ2_XS ([MODELS.md](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/blob/main/docs/MODELS.md)); 51.2B at ~4.5 bpw ≈ 28.8 GB, matching Strata's "28.8 GB n-gram table kept on SSD, read a few rows per token through the OS cache" ([HOW_IT_WORKS.md](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/blob/main/docs/HOW_IT_WORKS.md)). So Strata already streams an Engram table from the slow disk at decode time. [I]

## 2. Paradigm survey

Bottleneck shorthand: **V** 8 GB VRAM · **B** RAM bandwidth · **D** disk cold start · **L** long context · **T** tool reliability. "Needs GPU" experiments wait for the bake-off to finish; "downloads" need owner approval.

### 2.1 Hybrid linear-attention / SSM
- **Mechanism [S].** Replace most attention layers with fixed-size recurrent state; keep a few attention layers. Mamba-2 ([2405.21060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21060), May 2024); Gated DeltaNet ([2412.06464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464), Dec 2024); Mamba-3 ([2603.15569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15569), 2026-03-16, ICLR 2026: trapezoidal discretization, complex state, MIMO; +0.6 pt over Gated DeltaNet at 1.5B, +1.2 more with MIMO); Kimi Linear ([2510.26692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26692), 2025-10-30: 48B/3B, −75% KV, up to 6× decode at 1M); Nemotron-H ([2504.03624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03624)), Falcon-H1 ([2507.22448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22448), parallel attention‖Mamba-2), Jamba ([2403.19887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19887)); IBM [Granite 4.0](https://www.ibm.com/new/announcements/ibm-granite-4-0-hyper-efficient-high-performance-hybrid-models) (Oct 2025, ~9:1 Mamba-2:attention, ">70% RAM reduction" is IBM's claim); [Nemotron 3 Nano](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20848) (2025-12-23, 31.6B/3.2B active, Mamba-2+MoE, 1M ctx). *(IDs for the older papers are from memory, not re-fetched.)*
- **Key fact [S]:** the incumbent is already a hybrid. [Qwen3.5-4B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B) (Feb 2026, Apache-2.0) is 8×(3 Gated DeltaNet + 1 gated attention), 4 KV heads, 262K native ctx, MTP-trained.
- **Predicts for Haven [I].** L and V: only 8 of 32 layers hold KV, so 128K context costs roughly a quarter of a dense transformer's cache. Little effect on D, B or T. Haven's 91 tok/s on the 4B shows Gated DeltaNet runs acceptably on sm_75 in the pinned llama.cpp [S: Haven receipt].
- **Maturity.** Qwen3.5, Granite 4.x, Nemotron 3 Nano and Falcon-H1 have GGUFs; Mamba-3 has no released LLM checkpoint I found.
- **Experiment H1 (no downloads, needs GPU).** `llama-bench` Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M at ctx 8K/32K/128K; record VRAM and prefill/decode tok/s. *Predicted:* total VRAM at 128K ≤ 6.5 GB and decode at 128K ≥ 60% of decode at 8K. **Kill:** OOM at 128K or decode < 40% of the 8K rate, in which case "hybrid solves long context on 8 GB" is false on this card.

### 2.2 Liquid AI LFM2 and liquid/CfC networks
- **Mechanism [S].** [LFM2 report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404) (2025-11): hardware-in-the-loop search yielded gated short convolutions + a few GQA blocks (16 blocks; 10 conv / 6 GQA is 2ndary), 350M–2.6B dense plus 8.3B/1.5B-active MoE, 32K ctx, up to 2× faster CPU prefill/decode. **LFM2 is not a CfC/ODE network**; the closed-form-continuous-time lineage (Hasani et al.) did not scale into an open LLM I could find. [I]
- **Predicts [I].** CPU-friendly small router/worker that leaves the GPU alone (V, B). No lever on T or D.
- **Maturity.** Weights and llama.cpp support exist since 2025; license is `other` (LFM Open License), held for review (peer scout refresh, [PR #47](https://github.com/erpzz/haven/pull/47)). The bake-off covers its quality tonight, so I do not duplicate it.
- **Experiment L1 (CPU only, downloads).** LFM2-1.2B Q4 on 6 CPU threads as a *tool-selection-only* router on the frozen suite. *Predicted:* ≥ 25 tok/s on CPU and ≥ 85% of the 4B's tool-choice accuracy (not full task pass). **Kill:** < 70% of the 4B's choice accuracy.

### 2.3 RWKV-7
- **Mechanism [S].** [RWKV-7 "Goose"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14456) (2025-03-18): generalized delta rule, vector-valued gating, constant memory per token; 2.9B released Apache-2.0; llama.cpp support merged Mar 2025 with CUDA ([wiki](https://wiki.rwkv.com/inference/llamacpp.html), commit mirror, 2ndary).
- **Predicts [I].** The distinctive win for Haven is not speed but **tiny snapshot-able state**: per-user conversation state of megabytes instead of a multi-GB KV cache, so a "resume" avoids re-prefill (L, D). Quality at ≤3B is unlikely to match Qwen3.5-4B on tool contracts.
- **Experiment R1 (downloads, needs GPU).** RWKV-7 2.9B Q4: save state after a 16K prompt, restart, restore, and time to first token vs re-prefill. *Predicted:* state < 150 MB, restore < 1 s vs ≥ 20 s re-prefill. **Kill:** state > 1 GB or restored outputs diverge from uninterrupted greedy output.

### 2.4 Test-time memory / training
- **Mechanism [S].** Titans ([2501.00663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663), 2024-12-31) learns a neural memory updated by "surprise" at inference; ATLAS ([2505.23735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23735), ICML 2026) optimizes over a window; Nested Learning ([Google Research blog, 2025-11-07](https://research.google/blog/introducing-nested-learning-a-new-ml-paradigm-for-continual-learning), NeurIPS 2025); TTT-E2E ([2512.23675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23675), Dec 2025: sliding-window transformer that keeps training on its context, 3B, constant latency, 2.7× faster than full attention at 128K, code public). All numbers are the authors'.
- **Predicts [I].** Would address L and "private long-term memory" with no growing KV, but needs gradient steps at inference (compute and VRAM on a 2070S), has no llama.cpp path, and no ≥3B instruction-tuned checkpoint I could find.
- **Maturity.** Research code only. **Watchlist, not an experiment target.** Trigger: open ≥3B chat checkpoint plus an inference path.
- **Deployable proxy H4 (needs GPU).** Haven-style long-memory episodes: Qwen3.5-4B with 32K raw context vs 4K + retrieval over notes. *Predicted:* retrieval ≥ raw on recall at ≤ 40% of the prefill time. **Kill:** retrieval loses > 10 points of recall.

### 2.5 MoE expert offload, caching, and Engram
- **Mechanism [S].** Experts live in RAM, hot ones cached in VRAM. Mainline llama.cpp gained `--moe-cache-mib`, an LRU GPU expert cache, [PR #29887](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/29887) merged 2026-10-07 (author's Qwen3.8-Flash-Next Q4_0 numbers: 4090 25.0→40.7 tok/s at 77% hits, 5090 30.8→67.8 at 89%; 3090 only 1–5% over a tuned `--n-cpu-moe`; author says they will not continue the feature); academic prediction/prefetch: [ProMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22134), [ExpertFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17954) (claims 91.96% hit ratio), [MoBiLE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12357), and a [counterpoint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16473) that CPU-side expert compute can beat PCIe weight transfer. Engram ([2601.07372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07372), 2026-01-12; v2 2026-07-12): O(1) hashed N-gram lookup as a second sparsity axis, +3.4 MMLU / +5.0 BBH over an iso-FLOP MoE at 27B, and deterministic addressing "enables runtime prefetching from host memory, incurring negligible overhead" [S, abstract].
- **Predicts [I].** Decode ceiling is RAM bandwidth ÷ active bytes: 6B active × ~2.3 bpw ≈ 1.7 GB/token; DDR4 dual channel at ~40–50 GB/s gives a ~25–30 tok/s ceiling, close to the observed 27.7 (GPU cache hits lift it). Because Engram indices depend only on raw tokens, they can be prefetched; Strata's reading them "through the OS cache" from a 40 MiB/s disk is a latent risk when the page cache is cold. **Disk misses are fatal:** an IQ2_XS expert is ~1.4 MB (35.5 GB / 24,576), about 35 ms at 40 MiB/s; a 5% miss rate on ~480 expert slots per token is ~0.8 s/token. So "start serving before everything is loaded" does not work on a slow link.
- **Maturity.** Strata's signed engine includes sm_75 ([STRATA_LINUX.md](../../ops/haven-core/STRATA_LINUX.md)); llama.cpp cache is mainline but its authors report no benefit on well-tuned Ampere. Unknown on Turing; measure.
- **Experiment M1 (needs GPU; downloads).** Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (Apache-2.0, 2026-04-15, [2ndary](https://docs.apiyi.com/en/news/qwen-3-6-opensource-launch)) at a ≥4-bit quant (~21 GB, fits wholly in RAM) via mainline llama.cpp `--cpu-moe --moe-cache-mib` ≈ 5 GB, versus Strata IQ2_XS. *Predicted:* 18–30 tok/s decode, ≥ 76/110 on the frozen suite. **Kill:** < 12 tok/s or < 70/110. If it passes, "RAM-resident 35B at 4-bit beats 125B at 2-bit" and the Strata giant is not worth its footprint.

### 2.6 Low-bit and QAT on Turing
- **Mechanism [S].** BitNet b1.58 2B4T ([2504.12285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12285), Apr 2025) native ternary; [bitnet.cpp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11880) CPU kernels; ParetoQ ([2502.02631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02631), NeurIPS 2025): ternary/2/3-bit QAT beat 4-bit on size-accuracy at ≤3B, 3B ternary 4.1 pts from fp; EfficientQAT ([2407.11062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11062)): 2-bit Llama-2-70B −2.9 pts.
- **Predicts [I].** Bytes matter for D, B and V, but Turing has no BF16/FP8, so gains come from integer/dp4a-style kernels only; I found no verified sm_75 ternary CUDA path. A 2B ternary model is a speed/size curiosity, not a capability lever. The relevant question is whether Strata's 55/110 reflects 2-bit damage or behavior.
- **Experiment Q1 (folds into M1).** If the 35B-A3B at Q4 scores ≥ 76 while Strata scores 55, quantization+behavior is the cause; also run the same 35B at IQ2_XS. *Predicted:* Q4 − IQ2_XS ≥ 8 points. **Kill:** < 3 points (then bit-width is not what limits tool scores and QAT is the wrong lever).

### 2.7 Speculative and multi-token decoding
- **Mechanism [S].** Lossless: draft tokens verified in one pass. llama.cpp merged native MTP, `--spec-type draft-mtp`, [PR #22673](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673) on 2026-05-16 (Qwen3.6 27B and 35B-A3B tested; ~72–83% acceptance; 1.85× on 3090+3060; ~2.5 GiB extra VRAM; prompt processing roughly halves; a 6 GB-VRAM laptop gained 22.9→29.4 tok/s on the 35B-A3B). Google's [Gemma 4 MTP drafters](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/mtp/overview) claim up to 3× and note the 26B-A4B drafter "may not yield speedups" without good parallelism. Strata exposes MTP; Haven ran it off ([receipt](../../ops/haven-core/linux-strata-result-20261026.json)).
- **Predicts [I].** Dense, bandwidth-bound decode wins most: verification reads weights once. Offloaded MoE wins less, since a multi-token verify touches the union of experts and the miss path is the bottleneck. Hits B and D-adjacent latency, not T.
- **Experiment S1 (needs GPU; MTP GGUF may need a download).** Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M, `draft-mtp` n=2 vs off, greedy, 110 frozen episodes. *Predicted:* 1.25–1.5× decode, byte-identical outputs. **Kill:** < 1.15× or any output divergence under greedy.

### 2.8 Diffusion language models
- **Mechanism [S].** Parallel denoising of a block: [LLaDA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992) (Feb 2025), [LLaDA2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15745) (2025-12, 16B/100B MoE), Mercury (commercial, API-only, 1000+ tok/s on datacenter GPUs, self-reported). DiffusionGemma 26B-A4B (block-diffusion, 256-token canvas; "2000+ tok/s" on an RTX 6000; behind Gemma 4 on AIME 2026 69.1 vs 88.3; needs unmerged llama.cpp PR #24423) per [Unsloth docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/diffusiongemma), 2ndary.
- **Predicts [I].** Diffusion converts memory-bound decode into compute-bound passes, a fit for dense models on a GPU, a poor fit for offloaded MoE: a 256-token block's expert union covers most of the 26B, so each denoise step could read most of the weights from RAM. Lower reasoning scores hurt T.
- **Maturity.** Dream/LLaDA run in mainline `llama-diffusion-cli`; best open large model not merged.
- **Experiment D1 (low priority, needs GPU).** Dream-7B or LLaDA-8B Q4 vs Qwen3.5-4B on 20 tool episodes. *Predicted:* ≤ 1.5× the 4B's tok/s and < 40/110. **Kill:** that outcome; park until a ≥4B diffusion model with tool training lands in mainline.

### 2.9 Small model + tools/retrieval vs one big model
- **Source [S].** NVIDIA, [Small Language Models are the Future of Agentic AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02153) (2025-06): SLMs suffice for most agent calls; heterogeneous systems for the rest (position paper).
- **Haven evidence [S+I].** 4B 76/110 vs 125B-class teacher 55/110 on one synthetic suite, single seed. Strata solves `discover_then_state` 10/10 but fails execution-refusal. This supports cascade over replacement, but the suite is synthetic.
- **Experiment C1 (no GPU, free).** From private per-episode results, compute the *oracle union* of 4B and Strata passes, then a confidence-threshold cascade. *Predicted:* union ≥ 90/110 and a realistic cascade ≥ 82/110 with ≤ 25% escalations. **Kill:** union ≤ 80/110 (Strata adds nothing; drop it from routing).

### 2.10 Newer in 2026 that moves the picture
Gemma 4 (2026-04-02, Apache-2.0, 26B-A4B; [2ndary](https://the-decoder.com/googles-gemma-4-is-now-available-with-apache-2-0-licensing-for-the-first-time/)); Qwen3.6 (2026-04); DeepSeek V4 (reported 2026-04-24, Engram/mHC lineage; specs conflict across blogs, unverified); llama.cpp MTP (May) and MoE cache (Oct); Engram arriving in an open model (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, Aug). The pattern: **MoE + linear-attention + MTP + lookup memory has become the default open recipe**, so Haven's job is the I/O and routing around it.

## 4. What counts as a breakthrough

- **Real breakthrough (needs ≥ 3 runs, frozen suite):** one configuration that beats the 4B on capability **and** meets interactive latency: ≥ 90/110 on tool tasks (the 4B has 76), ≥ 20 tok/s, ready ≤ 60 s, running inside 8 GB + 64 GB. Or a long-context/memory design with per-user resume < 2 s and < 1 GB state at equal recall. Nothing surveyed has shown that yet. The Engram + MoE + hybrid class is the only credible candidate because it separates knowledge from compute. [I]
- **Incremental:** speedups of the same model (MTP, expert cache, faster link), a smaller footprint at equal score, or new SFT on the 0.6B. Worth doing; not a paradigm shift. The cold-start fix (#1) is the biggest *practical* win and a six-fold one, but it is plumbing.
- **Not evidence of anything:** vendor tok/s on datacenter GPUs, abstract-level benchmark deltas, and any Haven number from one seed on a synthetic suite.

## 5. Caveats
Several 2026 facts come from blogs and aggregators (marked 2ndary). The Qwen3.8 specs are NVIDIA docs plus a press article, not a Qwen release page. Older arXiv IDs were not re-fetched. All predicted numbers need a first run before anyone relies on them. Nothing was executed on Haven hardware for this document.
