# Observatory opportunity backlog

All entries below are **design proposals from the discussion**, not implemented capabilities, external research findings or accepted requirements. The IDs are R15-local discussion anchors; they do not renumber the existing REQ/AT/EX registries. The R15 researcher should expand and challenge them, with evidence, costs and falsifiable experiments.

| ID | Ability and illustrative interaction | Why it belongs | First evidence or design gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| OBS-01 | World inspector: select an observation, device or artifact and ask what it is, when it was observed and what supports it. | Connects system administration, mapping and physical instrumentation. | Trace every displayed claim to a versioned source; distinguish a rendered object from an enrolled physical asset. |
| OBS-02 | Planet-to-workbench navigation: region → local observation → instrument → fixture → CAD revision → experiment. | Makes the engineering agent part of the world interface. | Demonstrate one complete lineage using synthetic/public records without claiming geometric registration that was not measured. |
| OBS-03 | Time machine: replay what happened and separately what Haven knew at that time. | Debugs evidence delays, corrections and decisions. | Represent event, capture, receipt and correction times without inventing unrecorded history. |
| OBS-04 | Honest fog of war: expose recent, old, occluded, inferred and unmeasured space. | Connects RF research to meaningful uncertainty rather than a decorative heatmap. | Users can distinguish unknown from empty and learned completion from measured geometry. |
| OBS-05 | Active-observation planner: compare the value of one more handheld, fixed, rover or aerial observation. | Gives sensors and robots a question to answer. | Use a finite candidate set and compare against a simple baseline; retain separate acquisition and movement permissions. |
| OBS-06 | Owned-resource board: inspect capabilities, readiness and limitations of devices and compute workers. | Offers a strategy-game-style view of genuinely usable equipment. | Unknown calibration, stale state or simulated identity must not appear ready for real dispatch. |
| OBS-07 | Scenario fork: change a relay, route, light angle, resource or sensor placement in an isolated copy. | Connects simulation interests to practical planning. | No scenario path reaches live adapters; assumptions and differences are inspectable. |
| OBS-08 | Engineering technology tree: show experiments needed to qualify the next ability. | Makes progress and failed experiments visible rather than hiding them in chat. | A status change requires the appropriate evidence/reviewer; a new design never qualifies itself. |
| OBS-09 | Cooperative field view: deliberately share selected pins, short messages or directional cues. | Connects phone, glasses and silent-communication ambitions. | Each participant controls audience and retention; native glasses support remains device-specific. |
| OBS-10 | Public regional observatory: ask what changed in weather, water, terrain imagery or documented transport conditions. | Extends usefulness beyond the household into science and local awareness. | Display source resolution, refresh limits, coverage and age; correlation is not causal attribution. |
| OBS-11 | Portable field kit: eligible offline maps, local notes and queued reports with delivery states. | Supports expeditions and outages. | Offline rights are verified; local storage and internet delivery remain distinct. |
| OBS-12 | Transparent job board: show sponsored research/analysis, cost allowance, cancellation and results. | Makes autonomous workers useful and accountable. | No fictional perpetual thinking, unbounded watchers or private reasoning logs; no model per map object. |
| OBS-13 | Cinematic evidence director: source-linked tours and after-action replays. | Preserves the fun and makes results understandable to others. | Clear LIVE/REPLAY/SCENARIO labels, visual-style disclosure and private-data stripping before export. |
| OBS-14 | Network-dependency atlas: inspect how owned relays, services and local paths depend on each other. | Connects networking expertise to outage and field-communications experiments. | Separate configured topology from measured connectivity and modeled radio coverage; do not scan unowned networks. |
| OBS-15 | Temporary citizen-science station: combine a small set of enrolled instruments around a finite research question. | Provides a path to shared scientific utility and meaningful robotics tasks. | Consent, placement, calibration, retrieval, source rights and finite collection limits are explicit. |

## Five concepts to deepen first

Start from OBS-01/02, OBS-03, OBS-04/05, OBS-07 and OBS-08. Treat the combinations as five coherent experiences rather than duplicate features. Compare their usefulness, technical dependency count and cost, then revise the priority order with evidence.

## Cumulative connections

R02 supplies bounded jobs, context and eligible model routing. R03 owns identity/consent/publication semantics. R04 and R14 inform wearable/private communication. R06/R07 supply spatial support, frames and world-model rules. R08 supplies engineering lineage and independent evaluation. R09/R10 supply qualified resource and motion interfaces. R11 owns communications and emergency boundaries. R13 evaluates claims. None is replaced by a globe, a new visual label or a universal admin endpoint.

The source registry and upstream audit must establish what already exists in GEV or another project. Do not claim these combinations are unprecedented without prior-art research. Keep simpler solutions, negative results and an explicit no-new-hardware path in the comparison.
