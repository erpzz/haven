## F01 — Positive no-dispatch evidence versus uncertain transmission

| User-visible wording | Required authoritative evidence | Permitted follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| “Saved here; not sent” | Exact local operation/output/destination revision has a durable local commit AND the authoritative attempt state positively establishes LOCAL_ONLY / NOT_DISPATCHED, with no admitted, issued, buffered or in-flight transport attempt and the relevant no-dispatch boundary closed/fenced | User may later explicitly request a new eligible action; the label itself grants nothing |
| “Not sent: [verified reason]” | Attributed terminal prevention/refusal explicitly proves no transmission for that exact operation at the controlled boundary | Show the reason and an explicit review path; do not infer this state merely from error/timeout |
| “Transmission may have occurred; delivery is unknown. Checking status only.” | A send/permit may have occurred, an acknowledgement is missing, or authoritative attempt/commit evidence is incomplete | Bounded read-only status lookup for the same immutable operation tuple; never automatically resubmit, remint a permit or create a replacement operation |
| “Delivery was reported; receipt by a person is unconfirmed.” | An authenticated, matching destination/adapter delivery observation | Preserve report attribution and limits; human acknowledgement is a separate fact |

An absent attempt row, absent ACK, timeout, local save, cancelled task or revoked grant is not positive no-dispatch evidence. Inability to establish the complete attempt state yields UNKNOWN. A local queue record can coexist with actual remote receipt. Already admitted/buffered/in-flight content cannot be labeled prevented based only on later cancellation. An explicit transport failure supports not-sent only when its semantics actually establish no transmission; an opaque error does not.

Preserve four distinct facts from accepted P01: immutable release authorization; immutable consumed-permit receipt; append-only delivery observations; current future eligibility. The UI projects these facts and cannot authorize operations. Later revocation/cancellation fences future eligibility, but leaves prior sent/unknown/reported delivery history intact. Suggested combined wording: “Delivery of the earlier attempt is unknown. Further sharing is now blocked.” Do not promise recall or erase possible disclosure. Reconciliation is itself access-controlled and content-minimal; if it cannot run under current policy, report status unavailable and keep uncertainty rather than retransmitting.

A late matching authenticated ACK can append a reported outcome without authorizing replay or proving human perception. Duplicate reports do not cause another send; mismatched/conflicting reports cannot settle the wrong operation and remain auditable. A separately explicit replay, if ever authorized, is a new current-policy operation; finite-use R03 rules may deny it. Historical records never serve as permission. No automatic quota refund, reset or second grant debit is introduced by this UI clarification.
