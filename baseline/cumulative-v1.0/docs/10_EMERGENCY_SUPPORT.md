# Emergency healthcare, fire/rescue and protective assistance

The aim is practical support: faster useful information, better communication, illumination, location context, access to selected supplies and resilient coordination. This prototype is not a medical monitoring service, autonomous rescue organization or fireground navigation authority.

## Independent response path

Separate the native/contracted human-help path from optional evidence gathering. If a qualifying workflow requests help, it must not wait for a model response, a drone launch, a new printed part or a cloud-spend decision. Maintain unambiguous local/requested/provider-accepted/person-acknowledged/actual-dispatch states. A local inbox record is not “help is on the way.”

The existing S2 standing-incapacitation-policy simulation remains a target. It uses synthetic qualifying events, a versioned policy, deadline, bounded mission, one-use limit and revocation. A random missed notification or a cheap-model health score cannot create this policy. Physical deployment comes after separate clinical/operational/device/regulatory review.

## Scenario design

| Scenario | Useful first response | Optional later physical contribution | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explicit request for medical help | Assist communication and concise authorized information | Bring a phone/light/predefined kit on a qualified route | No diagnosis or improvised treatment decision |
| Qualified native alarm | Independent alarm/help path | Authorized outside observation or additional safe view | No delay for AI or drone evidence |
| Fire/smoke concern | Available public/authorized information; appropriate native help route | Ground/airborne lighting or observation only under qualified conditions | No automatic plume entry or interference with responders |
| Flood/blocked road | Existing reports/views and evidence uncertainty | Safe permitted exterior viewpoint | Image appearance does not establish safe driving depth |
| Missing consenting household member/pet | Authorized last-known information and human coordination | Search support under reviewed scope | No covert tracking/biometric identity/pursuit |
| Remote connectivity outage | Local information, stored messages and explicit upstream state | Qualified relay/data ferry | Local acceptance is not external delivery |
| Power outage | Readiness/status, existing light/communication paths | Bring/deploy approved low-risk equipment | No unqualified wiring or battery improvisation |

These examples are proposed workflows, not medical or tactical operating instructions. Real incidents require appropriate emergency services and competent responders. Test using synthetic scenarios and controlled safe fixtures.

## Healthcare information packet

A proposed packet includes incident ID, subject-consented identity/contact details, stated concern, location with time/uncertainty, measurements with original sample time/source/units, confirmed actions, missing information and a short human-readable summary. Do not transfer all health history by default. A model-generated explanation remains distinct from a reported symptom or sensor measurement.

Separate per-person ordinary summaries, partner-sharing grants and emergency disclosure policy. The same household relationship does not establish all three. After revocation, queued disclosures are re-evaluated. Shared speakers/glasses must not announce sensitive details to the wrong audience.

R16 provides an actual field-study example of drone AED delivery. The lesson for Haven is to evaluate the full chain from eligible request through access and human use, not just flight time. It is not justification for clinical efficacy claims or autonomous treatment. No drug/device delivery or invasive assistance is authorized here.

## Illumination and communication

A bright light or speaker can help a person be seen, hear an operator or obtain a better observation. R31 confirms the category exists in an enterprise system; it does not qualify a budget-aircraft payload. Prototype on a ground stand or rover first. Measure actual useful illumination/intelligibility, aiming, energy, heat, glare, nuisance and camera effects.

Treat a light as both assistance and an active-perception input. If a new angle resolves an ambiguity, retain before/after evidence and the actual light configuration. Do not sweep bright/strobing output toward people, traffic or aircraft. A speaker's statement must identify uncertainty and avoid impersonating responders.

## Readiness over improvisation

Keep known kit inventory, charge state, expiration/inspection where relevant, planned recipients, equipment location and manual alternatives. A printer may create organizers or tested mechanical accessories beforehand. Do not depend on printing a life-critical part during a crisis or let a language model redesign safety equipment in real time.

A rover delivering a kit is an ambitious later task: doorway access, dropped items, people/pets, stairs and a distressed recipient create separate hazards. Begin with soft/light inert objects, empty controlled routes and a physically present operator. Do not assume an indoor robot can carry a person or open every door.

## Qualification and evidence

Predefine usefulness metrics such as time to understandable human information, correct recipient acknowledgement, false alarms, missing-data visibility and independent-help continuity. Keep response-path tests separate from sensing-model accuracy. Add a human-factors review: can a stressed person understand the interface, cancel an error and know what has not happened?

Aviation and radio constraints [R32–R35], device manufacturing/clinical claims [R36], local permissions and professional coordination require separate reviews. The design preserves ambitious simulations and future collaboration while refusing to disguise a bench prototype as a qualified emergency service.
