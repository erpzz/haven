# Product charter: a companion, a perception system and an engineering partner

Haven should make ordinary life easier while developing a grounded understanding of the surrounding world. It is not just a drone dashboard and not just a chatbot with a large plugin list. It should help Eric and Kennedy individually and together, using only the data and authority each deliberately grants.

Its long-term physical capability is broader than flight: phones, smart glasses, stationary nodes, portable instruments, rovers, manipulators and aircraft can all gather evidence or carry out bounded tasks. A printer and workshop can help create missing interfaces and experimental equipment. Wireless reconstruction is a central research branch, while presence and localization are useful intermediate milestones—not a substitute for the eventual objective.

## What makes the concept distinctive

The proposed contribution is the integration of personal context, reliable action receipts, uncertainty-aware spatial evidence, active perception and a measured engineering loop into a household-scale platform. Existing work demonstrates pieces, not this specific assembled system. Novelty is a hypothesis to assess against prior art, not a label earned by combining fashionable components.

The scientific goals are concrete: can inexpensive complementary observations improve location estimates on held-out rooms? Can an assistant choose a more informative next observation than a fixed scan schedule? Can a bounded engineering agent reduce fixture error over several physical iterations without relaxing its acceptance test? Can personal information remain private while two users share compute and selected household capabilities?

Everyday success also counts. Remembering a decision correctly, delivering a reminder after a restart, explaining stale evidence, or keeping an emergency message queued honestly can be more useful than an impressive but fragile demonstration.

## Design values

Preserve user agency; make uncertainty visible; own durable state independently of models; use the smallest capable model or no model; use supported interfaces when practical; design for graceful loss of internet, inference and individual devices; measure readiness; earn physical autonomy through qualification; keep useful failures and negative experimental results.

The name Haven evokes dependable support and a home that helps rather than surveils. It is not a clinical claim, a claim of sentience, or an exclusive emotional relationship. The assistant may be personable without theatrics, fear-based prompts or invented certainty.

## Product promises versus research aims

The first daily release can aim for useful conversation, sourced project recall and explicit drafts. The current M0 proves only durable request receipt. Later experiments explore voice, available wearable data, geometry, RF localization, embodied tasks and fabrication. No roadmap date guarantees universal through-wall imaging, autonomous rescue or medical diagnosis.

No raw employer/customer data enters this personal project by default. No covert occupancy monitoring, confrontation, weapons, person pursuit or public emergency interference is required by the user's protective intent. A narrow legal or technical gate affects the relevant deployment; it does not cancel the research ambition or other branches.

## Outcomes worth pursuing

Less daily cognitive load; continuity across devices; understandable private health summaries; better shared situational awareness; more resilient communication during outages; physical help with simple tasks; tools that make experimentation cheaper; and trustworthy records of what actually happened. Possible life-changing value lies in greater independence, accessibility and faster useful information—not a promise that this prototype will save a life.
